# Exploratory Discovery Findings

## Status

**Research paused as of 2026-09-03.** This summary preserves early evidence
without implying that external validation has been completed.

Four prior internal interview summaries were reviewed: two Project Managers and
two R&D Leads from one organizational context. All evidence is
**Self-reported**. The interviews predated the current protocol, were not
consistently anchored in the last actual Go/No-Go review, and did not include an
ARI concept test or workflow observation.

The summaries therefore do not count toward the validation gates. H1-H4 remain
**Unvalidated**.

## Exploratory signals

These are follow-up signals, not validated patterns or prevalence estimates.

| Signal | Supporting summaries | Interpretation boundary |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Manual status work was described | 4/4 | Mostly outside release review. |
| Status crossed multiple people or tools | 4/4 | Not directly observed. |
| Blockers or stakeholders affected confidence | 4/4 | Not only Go/No-Go. |
| Research work was harder to estimate | 2/4 | Outside ARI's scope. |

The supplied quantitative notes covered different types of project-management
and status work. They did not isolate active Go/No-Go review, were not directly
observed, and are not published as a baseline. None measures ARI-assisted review
or supports a time-saving claim.

## Contradicting evidence

- Repository and CI monitoring was not consistently described as material. This
  weakens the assumption that GitHub navigation alone explains the workload.
- The largest burdens concerned reporting, meetings, status maintenance, and
  stakeholder coordination. ARI addresses only a narrower release-readiness
  subset.
- Suggested improvements varied and often concerned planning or personal task
  capture. Those are adjacent jobs, not evidence of ARI usefulness.

## Implications

1. Keep status reporting, estimation, task capture, and new integrations outside
   the ARI roadmap without stronger release-specific evidence.
2. If research resumes, separate active Go/No-Go reconciliation from broader
   reporting, meetings, waiting, and project administration.
3. Test evidence freshness and ownership explicitly because formal systems may
   not reflect the team's current understanding.
4. Treat the current four summaries as prompts for future research, not as a
   denominator for validation claims.
